## 2. LITERATURE REVIEW

### 2.1 Traditional Inventory Management

Inventory management is an important part of supply chain operations. Businesses must determine appropriate stock levels to satisfy customer demand while controlling purchasing and storage costs. Traditional inventory management approaches commonly use historical sales, average demand, reorder points and safety stock to support purchasing decisions. However, decisions based only on fixed demand estimates may not adequately represent fluctuations in future customer demand.

### 2.2 Inventory Management Under Demand Uncertainty

Customer demand is not always predictable. Variations in customer preferences, market conditions and purchasing behaviour can lead to uncertain demand. Excessive inventory can increase holding costs and tie up business capital, whereas insufficient inventory can result in unmet demand and shortage-related costs. Therefore, inventory decisions must consider the possible outcomes of uncertain demand rather than relying exclusively on a single estimated value.

### 2.3 Newsvendor Model

The Newsvendor Model is a classical single-period inventory optimization model. It focuses on determining an appropriate order quantity before actual demand is known. The model balances the costs associated with excess inventory against the costs associated with insufficient inventory. Arrow, Harris and Marschak (1951), in their work on Optimal Inventory Policy, examined inventory decisions under both deterministic and uncertain demand. This provides a theoretical foundation for the single-period inventory decision considered in this project.

### 2.4 Stochastic Optimization

Stochastic optimization is a mathematical approach used to identify effective decisions when some model parameters are uncertain. In inventory management, customer demand can be represented as a random variable using an appropriate probability distribution. The objective is to maximize expected profit or minimize expected cost across possible demand outcomes. This approach allows businesses to account for uncertainty when selecting inventory quantities.

### 2.5 Monte Carlo Simulation in Inventory Management

Monte Carlo simulation is a computational technique that uses repeated random sampling to estimate the outcomes of uncertain systems. In inventory management, simulated demand scenarios can be used to evaluate different purchasing quantities and estimate their expected financial performance. Widyadana, Tanudireja and Teng (2017) investigated Monte Carlo simulation for inventory policies under stochastic demand. Similarly, da Cruz, de Salles-Neto and Schenekemberg (2024) combined mathematical optimization and Monte Carlo simulation for inventory planning under uncertain demand.

### 2.6 Research Motivation

Based on these concepts, this project develops a stochastic optimization model for a hypothetical e-commerce business selling wireless headphones. Customer demand is represented using a probability distribution, and Monte Carlo simulation is used to estimate expected profit for different inventory quantities. The selected stochastic policy is compared with a deterministic inventory decision under common demand scenarios. Sensitivity analysis is also conducted to investigate the effects of changes in demand uncertainty and business costs.

The project therefore demonstrates how stochastic modelling and simulation-based optimization can support inventory decisions under uncertain customer demand.
